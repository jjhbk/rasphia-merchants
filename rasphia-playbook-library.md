# Rasphia Playbook Library

### High-ROI plays by pillar, for salons, clinics, and gyms/studios

Each entry follows the same shape so it can be used two ways: as sales collateral in merchant conversations, and as few-shot examples for the diagnosis engine.

**The structure:** Signals → Play → Why it's high ROI → What it costs → What good looks like.

**The house rule that makes these credible:** every vertical also gets a *"What to skip"* — the money-losing move we explicitly tell them not to make. This is the differentiator. Never drop it.

---

# 1. SALONS & PERSONAL CARE

**Business shape:** small catchment (1-3 km), high repeat potential, revenue capped by chair-hours, heavily reputation-driven, midweek troughs are the standard failure mode.

---

## GROWTH — get found, get chosen

### Play G1 · Lapsed-client win-back over WhatsApp
- **Signals that trigger this:** >100 customers with no visit in 4+ months; no existing re-engagement messaging; customer list exists (even messy).
- **Play:** Segment lapsed clients by last service and spend. Send a personal-feeling WhatsApp — not a discount blast — referencing their last service and a specific reason to return ("your last colour was in March, most clients rebook around now").
- **Why high ROI:** Reactivating a known customer costs a message; acquiring a stranger costs ad spend plus a trust gap. Highest-return play available to almost every salon.
- **Cost:** near-zero (messaging only). Optional small incentive on second tier of non-responders.
- **What good looks like:** 8-15% reactivation on the first send; measurable within 14 days.

### Play G2 · Local micro-influencer collabs (3-5 creators, 5k-30k followers)
- **Signals:** visually demonstrable work (colour, curls, bridal); weak or inconsistent social presence; catchment is dense/urban.
- **Play:** Trade service-for-content with 3-5 genuinely local creators. Require: geotag, service named specifically, and one before/after. Stagger over 6 weeks, not all at once.
- **Why high ROI:** Hair and beauty are visual and trust-based — one credible local creator outperforms broad paid reach for a fraction of cost, and the content is reusable.
- **Cost:** service cost only (₹0 cash in most cases).
- **What good looks like:** 20-40 profile visits per creator post; 3-8 bookings attributable per collab.

### Play G3 · Review engine (the compounding one)
- **Signals:** <50 Google reviews, or reviews older than 6 months; high walk-out satisfaction but no ask.
- **Play:** Automated post-visit message 2 hours after appointment, with a one-tap review link. Ask only satisfied clients (route unhappy ones to a private feedback path instead).
- **Why high ROI:** Reviews feed Google ranking, AI-assistant recommendations, and human trust simultaneously — one system, three channels. It also compounds: every month of running it raises baseline discoverability permanently.
- **Cost:** automation setup only.
- **What good looks like:** 15-25% of visits converting to a review; 4.6+ average maintained.

## MANAGE — keep them coming back

### Play M1 · Midweek slot-filling
- **Signals:** Tue/Wed utilisation below 50% while weekends run full.
- **Play:** Auto-offer unfilled midweek slots 48h ahead to regulars who normally book weekends, with a modest midweek-only value-add (not a price cut — a free add-on treatment).
- **Why high ROI:** Fills otherwise-dead capacity at near-zero marginal cost, and crucially does *not* train weekend clients to expect discounts.
- **What good looks like:** midweek utilisation +15-25 points within 6 weeks.

### Play M2 · Rebooking-at-checkout habit
- **Signals:** average visit gap significantly longer than service-appropriate interval (e.g. 10 weeks for a 6-week colour).
- **Play:** Book the next appointment before the client leaves the chair, with an automated reminder 5 days prior and easy reschedule.
- **Why high ROI:** Shortening the average visit gap from 10 weeks to 7 is a ~40% revenue increase per client with zero new customers acquired. This is usually the single largest hidden lever in a salon.

### Play M3 · Customer support automation
- **Signals:** owner answering the same DMs all day ("open Sunday?", "price for balayage?", "do you do keratin?").
- **Play:** Auto-responder on WhatsApp/Instagram handling the top 10 repeat questions, escalating anything real to the owner.
- **Why high ROI:** Recovers 1-2 hours of owner time daily and captures enquiries that arrive after hours — the ones currently lost to whoever replies first.

## PAYMENTS — get paid faster, and more

### Play P1 · Deposit-backed bookings for high-value services
- **Signals:** no-show rate >10%; long-duration services (bridal, colour, keratin) being lost to no-shows.
- **Play:** Small refundable deposit (10-20%) via payment link at booking, applied to the final bill.
- **Why high ROI:** A no-show on a 3-hour colour slot is unrecoverable revenue. Deposits reduce them sharply and cost nothing to implement.

### Play P2 · Prepaid service packages
- **Play:** "5 cuts for the price of 4," "3-session colour package" — paid upfront via link.
- **Why high ROI:** Converts uncertain future revenue into cash today, and structurally locks the client out of switching salons for months. Improves cash flow and retention in one move.

### Play P3 · Instant payment links + one-tap tipping
- **Play:** Payment link sent at checkout; tip option included.
- **Why high ROI:** Removes counter friction, and digital tipping typically lifts staff earnings — a retention lever for *staff*, which for salons is often the real churn risk.

## ❌ WHAT TO SKIP — salons
**Don't run broad Meta/Google ads before win-backs, reviews, and rebooking are running.** A 2 km catchment means most of the audience you pay to reach can never realistically become customers. Ads are the *last* lever here, not the first — and only after retention is fixed, or you're paying to fill a leaking bucket.

# 2. CLINICS (DENTAL, GP, SPECIALIST)

**Business shape:** high-consideration decisions, research-first patients, episodic rather than habitual visits, follow-up compliance is both clinical and commercial, insurance/billing friction is real.

---

## GROWTH — get found, get chosen

### Play G1 · Search + AI-assistant visibility (the foundation)
- **Signals:** absent or thin Google Business Profile; no structured service/procedure information; not surfaced when assistants are asked "best [procedure] near [area]".
- **Play:** Complete structured presence — procedures offered, practitioner credentials, pricing ranges, insurance accepted, hours — published in machine-readable form and kept current.
- **Why high ROI:** Patients research before they call. Invisibility at the research stage makes every other rupee of marketing spend less effective. This is the prerequisite play, not an optional one.
- **What good looks like:** appearing in assistant answers and local pack for 3-5 core procedure queries within 60-90 days.

### Play G2 · Review generation with clinical sensitivity
- **Signals:** <30 reviews, or a rating below 4.3.
- **Play:** Post-visit review request sent only for routine/positive-outcome visits, never for sensitive procedures or difficult outcomes. Private feedback route for dissatisfaction.
- **Why high ROI:** Rating is the single strongest conversion factor for a clinic choice, and it compounds across search, assistants, and word of mouth.
- **Care note:** never incentivise reviews and never solicit around adverse outcomes.

### Play G3 · Referral loop with adjacent practitioners
- **Signals:** narrow specialisation; low referral inflow from GPs/physios/other clinics.
- **Play:** Structured referral relationships with 3-5 complementary practices, with a clean referral-back channel and shared availability.
- **Why high ROI:** High-trust, high-intent inflow at zero acquisition cost — and structurally hard for a competitor to displace once established.

## MANAGE — keep them coming back

### Play M1 · Automated clinical follow-up sequences
- **Signals:** no recall system; patients disappearing after treatment completion.
- **Play:** Procedure-specific follow-up schedule — check-in at the clinically appropriate interval (post-op day 3, 6-month cleaning recall, annual review), automatically triggered.
- **Why high ROI:** Recall is the closest thing a clinic has to recurring revenue, and it improves clinical outcomes simultaneously — the rare play where the commercial and clinical incentives point the same way.
- **What good looks like:** recall attendance 40%+; measurable within one cycle.

### Play M2 · No-show reduction system
- **Signals:** no-show rate above ~15%.
- **Play:** Layered reminders (72h, 24h, 2h) with one-tap confirm/reschedule, plus deposits on high-value slots (see P1).
- **Why high ROI:** Empty chair time in a clinic is expensive — staffed, equipped, unrecoverable.

### Play M3 · Insurance & claims support
- **Signals:** patients delaying or abandoning treatment over claim confusion; staff time consumed by claim paperwork.
- **Play:** Pre-treatment coverage clarity (what's covered, expected out-of-pocket, documents needed) delivered before the patient decides, plus claim-status tracking.
- **Why high ROI:** Claim friction is a silent conversion killer — patients who'd have proceeded simply don't. Removing uncertainty converts already-interested patients at zero acquisition cost.

## PAYMENTS — get paid faster, and more

### Play P1 · Treatment-plan payment schedules
- **Signals:** high-ticket treatments (implants, orthodontics, multi-session plans) with visible drop-off at the quote stage.
- **Play:** Structured instalment schedules with automated collection, presented at the point of quote as the default option rather than a concession.
- **Why high ROI:** Converts patients who wanted treatment but stalled on lump-sum affordability. Typically the highest-value single play available to a clinic.

### Play P2 · Deposits on high-value appointments
- **Play:** Deposit at booking for long or specialist slots, adjusted against the final bill.
- **Why high ROI:** Same no-show economics as above, concentrated where the loss is largest.

### Play P3 · Transparent pre-treatment estimates
- **Play:** Automated written estimate with insurance split and expected out-of-pocket, before the appointment.
- **Why high ROI:** Reduces billing disputes, speeds collection, and — because pricing anxiety is a real barrier — improves case acceptance.

## ❌ WHAT TO SKIP — clinics
**Don't buy paid lead packages before search and AI visibility are fixed.** You'd be paying per lead for patients who would have found you free — and lead-broker patients convert worse and churn faster than search-originated ones. Fix the free channel first, then decide if paid is even needed.

# 3. GYMS & FITNESS STUDIOS

**Business shape:** subscription economics, churn is the entire game, January spike then attrition, member outcomes drive retention drives revenue — the three are inseparable.

---

## GROWTH — get found, get chosen

### Play G1 · Trial-to-membership conversion system
- **Signals:** trials/drop-ins happening but no structured follow-up; conversion below ~30%.
- **Play:** Same-day post-trial message, a personal note from the instructor, a specific goal-linked recommendation, and a time-boxed membership offer within 72 hours.
- **Why high ROI:** These people already walked in and liked it. The gap between "attended a trial" and "asked them to join" is the cheapest revenue in the building — and most studios simply never ask.
- **What good looks like:** trial conversion from ~25% to 45-60%.

### Play G2 · Member-outcome content + micro-influencer collabs
- **Signals:** visually demonstrable results; loyal small member base; weak reach.
- **Play:** Member transformation stories (with consent) plus 3-5 local creators doing genuine multi-week programmes rather than one-off visits.
- **Why high ROI:** Fitness sells on believable proof. A real 8-week member result outperforms any produced ad, and a creator who actually trains there is credible in a way a paid post isn't.

### Play G3 · Corporate & community tie-ups
- **Signals:** located near offices/residential complexes; capacity underused in specific bands (mid-morning, mid-afternoon).
- **Play:** Bulk/discounted corporate memberships or society tie-ups aimed specifically at filling off-peak bands.
- **Why high ROI:** Fills capacity that would otherwise be pure loss, and acquires members in blocks rather than one at a time.

## MANAGE — keep them coming back (this is where gyms live or die)

### Play M1 · Attendance-drop early warning + intervention
- **Signals:** members whose visit frequency has dropped >50% versus their own baseline.
- **Play:** Automatic flag at the drop, followed by a *human-feeling* check-in from their trainer — not a marketing message. Offer a schedule reset or a different class time.
- **Why high ROI:** Attendance collapse precedes cancellation by weeks. Intervening in that window is dramatically cheaper than winning the member back after they've quit — and this is the single highest-leverage retention play a gym has.
- **What good looks like:** monthly churn down 2-4 points; each point is direct recurring revenue.

### Play M2 · Goal-based programmes with visible progress
- **Signals:** members with no defined goal; no progress tracking; generic programming.
- **Play:** Goal set at onboarding (strength, weight, mobility, event-specific), a matching schedule, and visible progress milestones surfaced back to the member monthly.
- **Why high ROI:** Members who see progress renew. Members who can't tell whether it's working leave — usually silently. Progress visibility *is* a retention mechanism, not a nice-to-have.

### Play M3 · Trainer-matched recommendations & habit nudges
- **Play:** Match members to trainers/classes by goal and schedule; light habit nudges (streaks, weekly targets) — supportive framing, never guilt.
- **Why high ROI:** Trainer relationship is the stickiest bond in a gym; members who bond with a trainer churn far less. Nudges work if they encourage, and backfire if they scold — keep the tone warm.

### Play M4 · Renewal reminders + membership tracking
- **Play:** Renewal sequence starting 21 days out, with usage summary attached ("you trained 34 times this quarter") and a one-tap renew.
- **Why high ROI:** Passive expiry is a large, invisible churn source. Showing members their own usage before asking them to renew reframes the decision around value received.

### Play M5 · Meal/nutrition plan add-on
- **Signals:** members asking nutrition questions; goals that require diet compliance.
- **Play:** Goal-linked nutrition guidance as a bundled or paid add-on, delivered with the training plan.
- **Why high ROI:** Improves member results (→ retention), and adds a second revenue line from existing members with no acquisition cost.
- **Care note:** keep to general, non-clinical guidance; anyone with a medical condition or an eating-disorder history should be routed to a qualified professional, not an automated plan.

## PAYMENTS — get paid faster, and more

### Play P1 · Autopay / mandate-based recurring billing
- **Signals:** manual monthly collection; payment-related churn; staff time spent chasing dues.
- **Play:** Standing-instruction autopay (UPI mandate/card) as the default membership option.
- **Why high ROI:** Passive churn from "forgot to pay" simply disappears, cash flow becomes predictable, and staff stop chasing. Usually the single fastest financial improvement available to a gym.

### Play P2 · EMI on annual memberships
- **Signals:** high drop-off between quoting annual and closing annual; most members choosing monthly.
- **Play:** Offer annual membership split into no-cost or low-cost EMI, presented as the default rather than the exception.
- **Why high ROI:** Annual members churn far less than monthly members. EMI removes the lump-sum barrier that pushes people into the higher-churn monthly plan — you win on both revenue and retention.

### Play P3 · Freeze-instead-of-cancel + win-back pricing
- **Play:** Offer a paid or free short freeze at the moment of cancellation intent, and an automated win-back offer 60-90 days post-cancellation.
- **Why high ROI:** A freeze retains the relationship and the payment mandate; a cancellation loses both. Cheapest possible churn mitigation.

## ❌ WHAT TO SKIP — gyms
**Don't spend on acquisition while monthly churn is above ~7%.** Filling the top of a leaking funnel is the most common and most expensive mistake in this category — every new member costs money and leaves before paying it back. Fix attendance intervention, autopay, and renewals first; *then* scale acquisition, when a new member is actually worth more than they cost.

# USING THESE AS FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES

When feeding the diagnosis engine, keep the format identical: **Signals → Play → Why → Cost → What good looks like → and always a What-to-Skip.** Two rules that keep output honest:

1. **Never recommend more than 2-3 plays.** The value is prioritisation. A list of ten is what an agency gives you, and it's why nothing gets done.
2. **Always name the skip.** If the engine can't identify something the business should *not* spend on, it hasn't understood the business well enough to be trusted on what it should.

*All ROI figures and benchmarks here are directional examples for illustration — replace them with real numbers from your own client outcomes as soon as you have them, since real before/after data is far more persuasive than any estimate.*
